  • Took Vaanianuvaad to production as a fully featured video conferencing application built on an SFU (LiveKit / WebRTC). Every participant speaks their own language and hears every other speaker translated into theirs, with video, live captions and transcripts.
  • Connected the media server to the translation pipeline: each speaker's WebRTC audio feeds streaming ASR → NMT → TTS, and translated speech goes back as per-language audio tracks. One synthesis per language and voice is shared by every listener of that language.
  • Designed the shared real-time room engine behind three products, multilingual meetings, one-to-many live broadcasts and live interpretation. Model calls run concurrently but are emitted in spoken order, speakers are ordered by when they stopped talking, and every listener gets lossless delivery with captions ahead of audio.
  • Designed turn detection and sentence formation over streaming ASR (LocalAgreement-2 with a streaming segmenter) that cut fragmented and broken sentences in live transcripts by 62%.
  • Built meeting features end to end: voice cloning so translations keep the speaker's voice, continuous language detection, hand raise and host mute, LLM-generated minutes, and Sahayak, an in-app RAG help assistant.
  • Built the live translation pipeline behind BHASHINI's multilingual YouTube streams of the Prime Minister's Mann Ki Baat and other national programmes, streaming each address live in 10+ Indian languages.

Research and side builds

Open source · MIT

Textless speech-to-speech translation, Hindi → English

A direct speech-to-speech model with no text in the loop: HuBERT features quantised by k-means (k = 1000) into discrete units, a unit-to-unit Transformer encoder-decoder and a unit HiFi-GAN vocoder. Trained from scratch and benchmarked against SeamlessM4T-v2.

Built a parallel Hindi-English speech corpus by matching FLEURS utterances on shared FLoRes sentence IDs; length-bucketed unit extraction made preprocessing ~3× faster.
